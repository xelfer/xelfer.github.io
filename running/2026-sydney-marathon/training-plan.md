- **No tempo/threshold/marathon-pace — removed from the plan.** The shin injury came from speed, not mileage. All runs stay easy through to race day.
- Strength training continues — it's durability/muscle, not "running intensity."

## Core Principles
1. **Mileage before intensity** — build the aerobic base + long run first. One stress at a time.
2. **Long run is king** — the priority. Build progressively to ~30-32km. This is what gets you to the finish.
3. **No strides or sprinting, ever** — permanent (trainer). Protects the calf/Achilles.
4. **No tempo/threshold/MP/hill-reps — removed from the plan.** Speed caused the shin injury; every run is easy to race day.
5. **Easy means easy** — most volume 7:00-7:30/km, HR <145. No ego.
6. **Strength 2x/week** — single-leg, glutes, core, calves (controlled calf raises = rehab). Hip flexor priority.
7. **Calf-gated build** — grow the long run gradually; if the calf flares, hold or step back. Manage the niggle while building.
8. **Down weeks every 4th** — drop volume 20-25% to consolidate.
9. **Easy starts / negative splits** — fix the go-out-too-hard default.

## Cross-Training (optional cycling — Nick's Neo2T/ROUVY)
**Bike is the impact-free accelerator: adds aerobic base without loading the leg. Encouraged, with rules:**
- **Always EASY (Z2 by power/RPE, not running HR)** — it's volume, not intensity. ~30–60min.
- **Doubles OK on SHORT easy-run days** (run first / or AM run + PM spin). Also good on rest days as active recovery, and the **day AFTER the long run** to flush the legs.
- **Long-run day stays standalone** — don't double it.
- **Keep ≥1 genuinely easy/off day/week**; don't let stacked doubles leave the legs flat for the long run (the priority session). More ≠ better — see [[feedback_pacing]].
- Rides record to Strava (not Garmin) → add the date to `manual-completions.json` to tick the dashboard.

### 🦵 COLD-START WARM-UP PROTOCOL — ADDED Aug 24 (rehearsed accidentally on Mon Aug 24's 5km)
**Race morning is a cold-start trap and it is worse than a normal run.** Wake 4:45 → sit in a car and a train → hang
around St Leonards Park from 6:55 → **stand still in the chute 7:35-8:07 at ~13°** → gun. That's roughly three hours of
near-total stillness ending in thirty minutes of standing.

## Race Day Strategy (Sydney Aug 30)
**First marathon = finish strong. Goal 4:45. He is running with the official pace group, so the strategy is simpler
than it has ever been: sit with the flag, eat on the buzz, and make one decision at 32km.**
> **⚠️ REWRITTEN Aug 28 2026 around the PACER.** Everything before this was written for a solo negative-split race — a
> 7:00/km opening, settling to 6:45, pressing from 32km. **That is obsolete.** He confirmed the Chute E 4:45 pacer at
> the expo on Aug 27 and they run **EVEN EFFORT, not even splits**, at 6:45/km average. Telling him to hold 7:00/km for
> the first 10km would now mean deliberately dropping the group he has chosen to follow.
> **⚠️ THE BULLET LABELS BELOW ARE LOAD-BEARING.** `launcher.py::_SEGMENT_START_KM` maps `start-10km` / `10-21km` /
> `21-32km` / `32-42km` to the distances the coach speaks them at, and `_BRIEFING_ROWS` reads `goal` / `hr cap` / `fuel`
> for the start-line briefing. **Rename a label and that cue silently disappears** — which is exactly what happened
> before this rewrite: only one beat survived, so the 32km decision cue was gone.

- **Start-10km:** Get behind the 4:45 flag and stay there, never in front of it. Adrenaline, a downhill bridge and 20,000 people will all pull you forward, and this is the only part of the race where discipline changes the outcome. If you lose the group here you will not get them back. Expect the splits to jump around — even effort means about 7:00 up the climbs and 6:20 on the descents, and a slow split on a hill is the plan working.
- **10-21km:** Hold position and feed. Nothing else to do. Halfway is around 2 hours 22. Stop reading the pace field on your watch — with even effort it will say alarming things all day. Follow the flag and let the pacer do the arithmetic. If it feels easy here, that is exactly what it is meant to feel like. Do not spend it.
- **21-32km:** The hills are here and the descents are the real risk — even effort means genuinely quicker running downhill, and drifting is your documented failure mode. Stay level with the flag. This is the stretch where staying boring pays for the last ten kilometres.
- **32-42km:** This is the decision, and the only one you make all day. Beyond here is further than you have ever run. If you feel strong, leave the group and press — six twenty a kilometre from here turns four forty five into four forty. If you do not feel strong, hold what you have and let them go. Both are good races. If you are struggling, take the five hours — that is still a good first marathon and it beats walking from thirty five. Walking is not failing.
- **Goal:** 4:45, which is 6:45 per kilometre. Sit with the flag and it takes care of itself.
- **HR cap:** Under 168 until 30km, then let it climb. This is the governor. The 30km ran at 158 average, so 6:45 pace will sit in the low to mid 160s; if it is over 168 before 30km the pace gives, not the cap.
- **Fuel:** Ten feeds. A Maurten before the start, then every 30 minutes — Maurten, squeeze, caffeine, repeating. About three hundred grams on course, which is sixty three grams an hour. Three caffeine gels only, at ninety minutes, three hours and four thirty. The schedule is a default, not a contract — if your stomach complains, skip or halve the next feed, take water, and reassess at the one after. Drop the caffeine first.
- **Aid stations:** Water every 2-3km, Powerade at every 5km marker. Walk through for water — five seconds at 6:45/km costs nothing and beats inhaling it. Portaloos at every drink station.

### Goal: 4:45 — Nick's call

**4:45 = 6:45/km.** Nick's choice, made Aug 20, and the pacing above is built to deliver it rather than to hedge.

**What supports it:** the 30km ran 6:55/km continuous while under-fuelled at ~43g/hr, on a taper day. Race day adds
proper carb loading, ~65g/hr, ten days of shed fatigue, adrenaline and a crowd. His HM PR pace is 6:06/km, so 6:45 is
39 sec/km slower than half-marathon pace — a normal marathon-to-half relationship.

**What the risk is, stated once:** the build had no marathon-pace work by design, and his longest run is 30km. 6:45
will sit above the easy band, so **HR is the governor — under 165 to 30km.** If HR says no before 32km, the pace gives
way and the day becomes ~5:00. That is a fallback, not a failure.

**Not on the table:** sub-4 (5:41/km — faster than his HM PR pace, over twice the distance) and 4:30 as a late rescue.
At 4:45 pace he reaches 32km in 3:38, so 4:30 would need **5:12/km for the last 10.2km** — near 5k race pace, at the
end of a marathon. 4:30 is only reachable by starting at 6:24/km, which is a different race plan.
